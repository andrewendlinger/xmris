(diary-dev-diary)=
# A dev diary for xmris

:::{note} Status: planned — branch `feat/dev-diary-skill`
:::

Every significant change to xmris starts with a Claude Code planning session and ends with a plan
file: a precise, ordered list of files to touch and edits to make. That is exactly the right
artifact for *executing* the work and exactly the wrong one for *approving* it. Reviewing a plan
means reading twenty implementation steps and reverse-engineering the one decision underneath them.

Meanwhile these docs already have a pipeline that turns markdown into something worth reading. The
obvious move is to point it at the planning session.

:::{important}
Every significant change gets one **diary entry**, written twice: once from the approved plan —
short enough to read on one screen, and the thing you actually review — and once at the end,
reconciled against what was really built.
:::

(diary-dev-diary-two-readers)=
## Two readers, one page

The entry is worth writing twice because it serves two people who never meet.

| | Written | Reader | Wants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pass 1** | first commit on the branch | you, right now | the shape of the decision, without implementation weight |
| **Pass 2** | last commit on the branch | whoever asks *"why is it like this?"* in a year | the truth, including where the plan was wrong |

Pass 1 is deliberately thin. The plan file already owns the steps, and repeating them here would
just produce a second plan — the failure mode this is meant to replace. What the entry owes you is
the problem, the decision, the shape it takes, and an honest list of what is still only assumed.

Pass 2 is where it earns the name *diary*. Prose that turned out wrong gets corrected in place, so
the article never misleads — but the divergences themselves get their own closing section. A plan
that survived contact with the implementation unchanged is rare enough that pretending otherwise
would make every entry less useful.

(diary-dev-diary-graduation)=
## When a concept outgrows the diary

An entry is a dated record, not a reference page. When a decision produces a concept readers need
permanently — [the two domains](../explanation/domains.md), [the controlled
vocabulary](../explanation/vocabulary.md) — that concept graduates into its own explainer, and the
entry keeps only the story of arriving at it. Two entries a year apart may both touch the same
concept without either being its home.

:::{dropdown} Why not an ADR log?
Architecture Decision Records are a filing format: numbered, templated, context / decision /
consequences. They are excellent at being findable and poor at being read. These entries have to
survive being *the thing reviewed before work starts*, which means they have to be articles —
motivated, concise, willing to spend a paragraph on why the obvious approach fails. A template with
five mandatory headings produces neither.
:::

:::{dropdown} Why not just keep the plan files?
Plan files live outside the repo, are written for execution rather than comprehension, and are
never revisited once the work lands. Nothing about them survives to answer "why is it like this?"
six months later — which is the question the diary exists for.
:::

:::{attention} Assumptions to verify
- Splitting the entry skeleton into `templates/entry.md` keeps `SKILL.md` short without the two
  drifting apart.
- A ≤500-word budget is tight enough to force real editing, loose enough for a genuine design.
- Diary entries need an explicit exemption from *one home per concept*; without it, a concept
  recurring across two dated entries reads as a rule violation.
:::
